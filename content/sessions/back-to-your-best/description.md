## Who this is for

The player in a run of games that hasn't gone his way. A couple of bad ones, and it's carried into the next.

Your game has got smaller. The safer option. Doing a bit less. Not quite feeling like yourself out there.

## What's actually happening

Two things, and neither of them is about your ability:

- Playing to avoid a bad performance produces a worse one. Not safer — worse. This has been researched directly and found repeatedly: players of the same ability produce different outcomes depending on what they're playing towards, and the ones playing to avoid getting it wrong come out lower. The caution isn't protecting you. It's what keeps the run going.
- Your belief comes from your record of doing it. Not from what you're told, not from how you feel about it — from the doing. And it's specific to the situations you're actually in. Something goes wrong once, then twice, then a few more times. You lose the belief in it. So you stop trying it. Now you rarely get it wrong, because you hardly ever go near it. Nothing new goes into the record, so nothing shifts. That's why a run can sit on a player for weeks.

## What this session conditions

- Your full game going back in — all of it, in the situations that come up in your football every week
- The quality that makes you the player you are, showing in your football at the level you do it at your very best
- Wanting the ball, showing for it, playing what's on
- Staying in the passage all the way through — on the ball and off it, to the conclusion
- Holding all of that when the game resists — bigger game, more pressure, less time on the ball
- Your belief rebuilding off the back of the doing

## How it works

Guided visualisation and hypnotherapy get you immersed and fully in the game. Then you go to work.

Imagining yourself playing engages much of the same motor planning circuitry in your brain as playing does — so the football itself sharpens. And rehearsing yourself handling difficult moments well has been shown to raise a player's belief in what he can do.

Two things at once. Your game develops, and the belief comes off the back of it.

You're guided through it, then handed 45-second intervals to run your own passages of play, over and over, without my voice. Then I come back in.

## How to prepare

Have this ready before you start:

- What makes you the player you are. Your qualities. What you bring, what you're good at, what other people would say about your game.
- Don't go off your recent games. When the belief goes, you can start doubting the player you are — as if the ability has gone somewhere. It hasn't. Think about the best player you've ever been.
- The moments where that showed. In those games. Where you were on the pitch, what was happening, what you were doing.
- This might take a bit of work. When the games haven't been going your way, the good stuff is harder to get hold of — that's simply how memory works. Do it before you start the session, not during it.

Rehearse those moments at the level you do them at your very best. That's the level that gets conditioned.

Use it daily. Use it before training. Use it before a game.
