Some players are horrible to play against. Not dirty. Horrible.

You'll know one. The one who's just… there. Every time the attacker gets the ball, every time they turn, every time they think they've got a second — he's there again. And they come off having played badly, when in truth they never really had a chance.

That's what this session conditions in you.

## What's actually happening

Two things, and neither of them is about the tackle:

- You take their time — and their information. A player gets their picture before the ball arrives, by turning their head away from the ball to look around them. Researchers call it scanning. In Premier League match data, the players who scan more before receiving complete more passes — and when a defender is tight to them, they scan less. They receive with less of the picture than they should have, facing the wrong way. The pass that follows is worse. Nothing to do with how good they are.
- You take their attention. Players are at their best in the flow state — the zone, the blue performance state — where it's all automatic and instinctive. That needs their focus fully on their own game. Your intensity breaks it. The brain runs two attention systems: one they point themselves, and one that's grabbed automatically by whatever registers. Be on them all game and you become that thing. Then they start checking for you before the ball has even arrived. Attention comes in a fixed amount — everything that goes on you has come off their football.

## What you're conditioning

- Being dialled in and on the front foot — reading it, anticipating, waiting for your trigger
- Engaging the moment that trigger comes, with brilliant aggression and intensity
- Aggression and intensity always combined with intelligence and awareness — your positioning, knowing when to engage
- Staying in it all the way through to the end of the passage of play
- Doing it relentlessly — not a statement in the first five minutes, but the whole game
- Winning it clean — fouls hand the advantage straight back to them
- Thriving on it: knowing your opponent is thinking about you every time they're anywhere near the ball
- Not that you MIGHT be there. That you ARE there.

## How to prepare

Before you start, have it ready:

- The passages of play you're going to work on
- The game, and the opponent you're focusing on
- What you actually do in those moments — your positioning, when to engage. That comes from your own experience, and from your coaches. Speak to them about the specifics and bring those into your visualisations
- The more real the detail, the more powerfully this conditions.

Use it daily. Use it before training. Use it before a game where you know exactly who you're up against.
