## Who this is for

Centre backs, full backs, holding midfielders — anyone who has to win the ball in the air when it matters.

The long ball, the cross, the set piece. The striker leaning into you. The moment where you either attack it or it gets past you.

## What's actually happening

Winning in the air starts well before the jump.

- Reading it early. Expert players pick up the cues that tell them what's coming — the body shape of the player crossing, the flight of the ball — sooner than others. Seeing it early is what gives you the position and the run-up.
- Attention on the threat, not the ball. When a big striker is on you, part of your attention gets pulled onto him — automatically. Attention comes in a fixed amount. Every bit that goes onto him has come off the ball, your timing and your position.
- Commitment. A duel you go into half-hearted is one you've already lost. Aggression and intensity, combined with timing and awareness, is what wins it clean.

## What this session conditions

- Reading the flight of the ball early and getting into position before your opponent
- Attacking the ball — full commitment, at the highest point, every time
- Positive aggression — strong, physical, and clean, because fouls hand the advantage straight back
- Your attention on the ball and the space, not on the player you're up against
- Clearing your lines with purpose — height, distance, direction
- Staying in it after the header — the second ball, the next phase, right through to the end of the passage

## How it works

Guided visualisation and hypnotherapy get you immersed and fully in the game. Then you go to work.

Imagining yourself playing engages much of the same motor planning circuitry in your brain as playing does. Rehearsal that carries the actual setting and the actual feeling of the moment — the crowd, the contact, the pressure — has been shown to produce bigger improvements than rehearsal done calm and neutral.

You're guided through it, then handed 45-second intervals to run your own passages of play, over and over, without my voice. Then I come back in.

## How to prepare

Have this ready before you start:

- The situations where aerial duels come to you — goal kicks, crosses, set pieces, long balls in behind
- The opponents you're up against, especially the ones who are strong in the air
- Your positioning and your job in those moments. Talk to your coaches about the specifics

The more real the detail, the more powerfully this conditions.

Use it daily. Use it before training. Use it before a game where you know exactly who you're up against.
