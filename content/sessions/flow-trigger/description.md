Two words. One for when the ball is yours, one for when it isn't. That's your whole plan for ninety minutes.

Not because simple is easier. Because of what the research says happens when you give a trained player anything more than that to hold.

## What's actually happening

- Your football runs best without your attention on it. You've trained it to the point where it produces itself. That's not a compliment, it's how skill acquisition works — the same as driving.
- Put your attention back onto your own execution and it comes out worse. This has been measured directly. Skilled performers asked to attend to a component of their own action produce it worse than when their attention is elsewhere — and a distracting second task doesn't do the same damage. It isn't the pressure. It's the direction of the attention.
- So concentrating harder on your technique in a game makes it worse, not better. This is one of the most researched findings in performance and it's the opposite of what most players are told.
- But you can't play with an empty head. Something occupies it. Under pressure, attention drifts towards whatever is most obvious in front of you, or onto yourself. If you haven't chosen what your attention runs on, the game chooses for you.
- There is a third option, and it's the one this session is built on. A word describing the overall way you're playing does not put your attention on your technique. It points at the whole of your game at once. Skilled performers under pressure holding a word like that outperform ones holding a word about part of the movement — tested across basketball shooting, long jumping, golf putting and driving. Same amount in your head. Completely different result.

## Your Game Blueprint

- Two words. One for out of possession. One for in possession.
- Whether your team has the ball is the one thing you know instantly, in every second of a game, without checking. Nothing else on that pitch is that obvious. So that's what carries your words.
- You never decide which one you're on. The ball changes sides and you're on the other.
- A fifty-fifty isn't yours yet. You're winning it back.
- They're entirely yours. Nothing can stop you being aggressive. Nothing can stop you being sharp.
- Which is why they can't be a clean sheet or a goal. Those are great outcomes, and they're beyond your control.

## Choosing your two

Four tests. Each word has to pass all four.

- Is it about how I play, rather than a technique? "Aggressive" passes. "Low elbow" doesn't.
- Is it completely mine, whatever the game does? No score, referee or teammate can take it off you.
- Would you see it if you watched the game back? If it's invisible on the footage, it's a feeling, not a blueprint word.
- If I did it at a high level for ninety minutes, would I be a better player?
- Then check they're a pair — two genuinely different jobs. Aggressive and relentless is one word twice.

## Work either side

Aggressive · Brave · Sharp · Lead · Dominant · Relentless

## Words that don't work, and why it matters

- Everywhere — conditions chasing the game instead of playing your job. This is the response players reach for when things aren't going well, and it costs them more than they think.
- Focused or Confident — these describe the state of your head, not your football. They fail the third test, and in a game they turn into checking whether you're focused, which is attention on yourself. That's the exact thing this session exists to prevent.
- Winner, clean sheet, goal, assist — outcomes, not manner. Not yours to control.

## What this session conditions

- Your two words running as the default quality of your football, without you having to put them there
- The change of possession carrying you from one word to the other with no decision made and no checking
- Playing to your word at a high level, not at its most — aggression that wins the ball cleanly, not aggression that concedes free kicks
- Full involvement through the whole passage of play, on and off the ball, to its conclusion
- Your attention out in the game — the picture, the space, your next action — for the full ninety minutes

## How it works

- Guided visualisation and hypnotherapy get you immersed and fully in the game
- You put yourself into a passage of play from your own football, out of possession, and play to your first word exactly as it looks when you're at your very best
- The ball changes sides, and you're on your second word, right through to the end of the passage
- Then you go again
- You're guided through it, then handed 45-second intervals to run your own passages of play, over and over, without my voice — then I come back in
- Every rehearsal builds the pathway. That's how the brain develops anything, and it's why this works.
- Use it daily. Use it before training. Use it the night before a game.

## Before you start

- Have your two words chosen and tested against all four questions
- Have a passage of play in mind from your own football — where you were out of possession, won it back, and did something with it
- Know what your word looks like at its best. Picture that version, every time. The level you rehearse is the level that gets conditioned.

## Using it in a game

- Your words go in during the breaks. Walking to a restart. A goal kick. A stoppage. Going out for the second half.
- Not during the play. While the ball's live, your attention is on the football — that's the whole point.
- The conditioning is what makes them show up in the play without you putting them there.
