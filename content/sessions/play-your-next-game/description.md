Preparation for your next game specifically — running the patterns and passages of play you're most likely to experience in it, against the opposition you're actually facing, in the ground you're actually playing at.

## This isn't only a game day session

Yes, it works in the hours before kick-off. But that's not where most of the value is.

- For a Saturday game, the optimum is Thursday, Friday, and the day of the game
- Big game? You could run it all week
- Thursday onwards will be ideal most of the time, so you've got the other days for your other sessions
- You don't prep physically in the thirty minutes before kick-off. Same principle here.

## Why this goes further than patterns of play in training

- Many more run-throughs. More repetitions conditioning your movement, your anticipation, and everything else you do — so it's more effective and automatic come game day
- No physical load. All the repetitions, none of the cost
- Real opposition. In training you're working against the U18s or against cones. Here you put your next opponents in front of you and play against them
- The whole environment. The intensity. The crowd. The stadium. No cone or mannequin gets you primed like that
- And if you're not a pro, or you're not doing patterns of play in training, you still know the passages that come up most in your games. Those are the ones you work on.

## The unfamiliar ground

Your brain does not like the unfamiliar. A new ground, or a pitch you've barely played on, is a novelty at best and intimidating at worst — and either way, some of your brain's bandwidth gets taken up by the new environment.

So make it familiar before you get there:

- Get on YouTube
- Find images of the ground online
- Visit the ground yourself if you can
- Then when you've imagined playing there over and over — with that intensity, that hostility — there's much less that's unexpected on match day. Your brain recognises this pitch, this ground. And it's one you've played really well at, time and time again.

You step out feeling great and feeling focused, with your patterns and passages conditioned and automatic.

## The knock-on

You're practising your game. Doing what you do, over and over again.

So you're not only prepping for the next game — you're improving your overall game at the same time.

## What this session conditions

- Your role inside your team's patterns of play, right the way through to the end of the passage
- Anticipation — seeing it early, reading where the space is and where it's about to open up
- Your movement and awareness inside the pattern, coming out automatically and instinctively
- Playing your game in a hostile environment, whatever's happening around you
- Staying dialled in when the game isn't going your team's way
- Familiarity with the ground and the pitch before you've set foot on it

## How to prepare

Before you begin, think about the patterns or passages of play you're going to work on. It might be a few, or just one you really want to develop.

Have your ground ready too — the pitch, the stands, the crowd. Whatever you've been able to find.

This should be a staple in your weekly routine. Many of you will be using it numerous times.
