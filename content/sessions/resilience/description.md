## Who this is for

- Every player. Because every player gets hit with things that go against them.

Left out of the squad. A game that went badly. A decision that cost you. Someone doubting you, writing you off, telling you what you haven't got.

You can't stop those things happening. What this conditions is what you do in the seconds after.

## What's actually happening

- Something negative happens, and you get a negative thought. That part is automatic. Your brain's survival system responds to a setback before you've had any say in it. Nothing has gone wrong with you when that happens — it's the system doing its job.

What you do next decides what happens in your body. Sitting in it — going over it, turning it, replaying it — keeps the stress response running long after the event itself has finished. That's been measured directly: dwelling on a setback prolongs stress-related physiological activation, including cortisol output, well beyond the thing that caused it. The event lasts seconds. The response can last days, and it's the response that costs you the next training session.

Changing the meaning you give it changes the brain's response — physically. When you actively reframe a negative event, the control regions of the frontal lobe engage and the amygdala's response drops. Across 48 brain-imaging studies, the amygdala was the region that consistently changed. Not a feeling of being better about it. A measured reduction in the survival system's output.

Treating pressure as something that's working for you produces a different physiology than treating it as a threat. People who reframe stress arousal as functional show more efficient cardiac output and lower vascular resistance under the same stressor — a body set up to perform rather than to defend.

And this is trainable. Reappraisal gets measurably better with repeated practice. More than that: material that has been reappraised repeatedly still produces a reduced amygdala response a week later. It's conditioning, in exactly the way the rest of your football is conditioning.

## The question

- One question does the work:

How can I use this to empower me?

That's the whole mechanism, in a form you can actually use in the moment.

- It's not positive thinking. Positive thinking tells you it's fine. It isn't always fine.
- It's the reframe the research measures — changing what the event means, not pretending it didn't happen.
- It moves you from the thing something is happening to, into the thing doing something with it.
- It's available every single time, whatever has happened.

## What this session conditions

- The question arriving automatically, in the seconds after something goes against you
- Setbacks turning into fuel rather than into days of dwelling on it
- Staying available for the next session, the next game, the next opportunity — instead of losing them to the last one
- The bigger the knock, the more you take out of it
- Doubt from other people going straight into the tank
- The same response holding outside football — in your family, your school or work, everything else

## How it works

- This one breaks the usual format, deliberately.

- No 45-second intervals, no visualisation blocks to run on your own. My voice is with you the whole way through.
- Guided visualisation and hypnotherapy get you deeply relaxed, then the conditioning goes in directly.
- You bring real setbacks to mind and run the question against them, guided, over and over.
- Then you rehearse it forward — against setbacks that haven't happened yet.
- Repeated reappraisal is what builds it. That's not a claim about this session; it's what the research on reappraisal training shows. Every run makes the response faster and more automatic.

## How to prepare

- Somewhere quiet where you won't be disturbed. Sitting or lying down, whichever you prefer.
- Have something real in mind before you start — something in your football that's gone against you recently, or something going against you right now. It doesn't have to be the biggest thing. It has to be real.
- Not while driving, training, or doing anything that needs your attention.
- Use it daily. Use it after a game that didn't go your way. Use it the day you get news you didn't want. And use it when nothing has gone wrong at all — that's when you're conditioning the response for when something does.
