Conditioning your interactions with one specific team mate — so you read them, find them, and play with them instinctively.

You'll know the connection this is talking about. Two players who look like they've got telepathy between them. One of them moves and the other one has already played it.

That's not telepathy. That's familiarity. They've played together enough times that they read each other without having to think about it.

This accelerates that — and makes it far more effective.

## The most important thing about this session

You're not thinking about a striker. Or a winger. Or a full back.

You're thinking about a specific player. A specific team mate. By name.

Every player is different:

- Different characteristics
- Different runs they make
- A different way they carry the ball
- Little tells in their body language that let you know where they're going to run, where they're going to pass, where they're about to look
- That's what you're dialling into. The position isn't the thing. The specific player is.

## What to expect from it

This is real and significant training, and you should expect it to show up in your numbers.

- A winger focusing in on their striker, working on assisting them more — expect your assists to go up, as you find them quicker, more often, and with better passes
- A centre back focusing on finding the winger with a diagonal ball — expect your successful long balls to them to go up
- Whatever the interaction is, that's the area of your game that improves.

## Why it works

The more you run this, the more of them gets conditioned into you — the subtle movements, the way they play, what they like to do.

And you're not looking for any of it when you're in a game. It's conditioned in the visualisations, and on the pitch it simply happens. Instinctively. Which is what makes it quick, and what makes it effective.

The right thing, more often. And a split second earlier.

That's a huge advantage.

## The knock-on

Every time you run this you're practising your own game as well — your movement, your decisions, your execution.

So you're building the connection with this player, and developing your overall game at the same time.

## What this session conditions

- Reading a specific team mate's movement — where they're going before they've gone
- Finding them earlier, more often, and with a better ball
- Receiving from them — knowing what's coming and being ready for it
- The interaction running automatically and instinctively, without you looking for it
- Holding that connection under pressure — bigger game, less time on the ball, hostile environment
- Your own movement, decisions and execution inside every passage of play, right through to the end of it

## How to prepare

Before you begin:

- Choose your team mate. One specific player
- Think about the interactions you want to work on — the passages of play where the two of you come together
- Know how they play. The more you know, the better this works. Watch them — in training, in games, in clips. How they move, how they carry the ball, as many of those little characteristics as you can bring into the visualisations
- Use it repeatedly. You might always focus on the same player, or you might have a few different players you cycle through.
