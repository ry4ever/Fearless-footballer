## Who this is for

The player who comes up against opponents who try it on. The mouth in your ear all game. The shirt pull, the elbow, the late challenge, the constant pushing and shoving off the ball.

You still play. But a big part of your attention is now on him — and your football has got smaller.

## What's actually happening

Two things, and neither of them is a choice you make:

- Your threat response takes your attention. There's a second system of attention running underneath the one you point yourself. Its job is to pull you onto anything registering as a threat — automatically, instantly. And attention comes in a fixed amount. Every bit that goes onto him has come off the game. You see less of the picture. Your decisions are a fraction slower.
- Your attention turns inward. How you're playing. Whether you look rattled. What your teammates are making of it. That's intrinsic focus — and it's the one that does the significant damage. Every best game you've ever played came with your attention pointed outwards, out in the game. Extrinsic focus. It's the way into the zone — the blue performance state. Turn it inward and your football stops being instinctive. Now you're the one putting yourself off. He's just started it.

## What you're conditioning

- Seeing his behaviour for exactly what it is — a routine, and a weak one, because you see straight through it
- His routine becoming the trigger that sends your attention straight back out onto the ball, the picture, your next job
- The reframe: the harder he goes at it, the clearer it is that he's worried about what your game does to him
- Doing the thing that actually hurts him — playing your game, relentlessly, exactly what he doesn't want
- Full involvement, unchanged — every duel, showing for the ball, all the way through to the end of the passage
- Staying in it when things aren't going your way — mistakes happen, your response is 100% yours
- This isn't ignoring him and it isn't going quiet. You still compete. What changes is where your attention sits while all of it is going on.

## How to prepare

Have your situations ready before you start:

- Games that have already happened where a player got to you
- Games coming up, and the players you know will try it
- What their move actually is — the mouth, the constant fouling, the pushing and shoving off the ball
